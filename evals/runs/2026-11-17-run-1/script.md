# Script: One trend pooled, another within species

## Opening

[S01] [goal] One dataset can show a correlation going one way when pooled, and the opposite way when split.
[S01] [goal] You will learn to read both and explain why.
[S01] [roadmap] We start with the pooled trend, then look within each species. {break}

## T1 · The pooled trend

[S02] [announce] First, the pooled trend.
[S02] [introduce] The question is whether penguins with longer bills also have deeper bills.
[S02] [introduce] The data are three hundred and forty two penguins of three species, from three islands of the Palmer Archipelago, Antarctica. {C07}
[S02] [introduce] They were measured from two thousand seven to two thousand nine. {C07}
[S03] [explain] Across is bill length; up is bill depth, the height of the bill. Both are in millimeters.
[S03] [explain] Each dot is one bird, and for now every bird is gray. {look:2}
[S04] [explain] The cloud tilts gently downward, and the correlation is weak and negative: r is minus point two four. {C01} {beat}
[S05] [wrap-up] So across all the birds, longer bills come with slightly *shallower* bills. {look:1}
[S05] [bridge] That pooled number mixes three species, so next we separate them. {C07} {break}

## T2 · Within each species

[S06] [announce] Next: within each species.
[S06] [introduce] The same dots now take one color and shape per species: Adelie, Chinstrap, and Gentoo. {look:1}
[S07] [explain] Start with the Adelies on their own. Their cloud tilts upward: r is point three nine. {C02} {beat}
[S08] [explain] Chinstraps tilt upward more strongly, at point six five. {C03}
[S08] [explain] Gentoos tilt upward at point six four. {C04} {beat}
[S09] [explain] Every species tilts upward, the opposite of the pooled sign. {look:1}
[S10] [explain] The reason is where each species sits. {look:1}
[S10] [explain] Adelies sit high and to the left, with short, deep bills. {C05}
[S10] [explain] Gentoos sit low: long bills, about forty seven millimeters, but the shallowest, about fifteen. {C05}
[S10] [explain] Chinstraps have long bills that stay deep, so they pull against the tilt. {C05} {beat}
[S10] [explain] Pooling puts short, deep Adelies beside long, shallow Gentoos, and that gap between species tilts the whole cloud downward. {look:2}
[S11] [explain] Gentoos also differ in size. They are the heavy species: about five thousand and eighty grams on average, against about three thousand seven hundred and thirty for Chinstraps. {C06} {look:1}
[S12] [wrap-up] Within each species, longer bills go with *deeper* bills; the pooled negative trend comes from differences between species. {C02} {C03} {C04}
[S12] [wrap-up] This reversal is called Simpson's paradox. {beat}
[S12] [wrap-up] These correlations describe how bill length and depth go together, not what shaped them.
[S12] [bridge] So one trend runs between species, and another runs within them. {break}

## Conclusion

[S13] [synthesis] Pooled, longer bills come with shallower bills. Within each species, they come with deeper bills.
[S13] [synthesis] Both are true: one describes the mix of species, the other the birds inside each.
[S13] [synthesis] So the first question for any pooled correlation is which groups it combines. {look:2}
