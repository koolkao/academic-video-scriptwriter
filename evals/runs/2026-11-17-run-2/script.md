# Script: Longer bills, shallower bills?

<!-- One line per spoken segment: [scene] [role] text {claim} {pause}. -->

## Opening

[S01] [puzzle] Across three hundred and forty two penguins, longer bills tend to be *shallower* bills. {C01} {C02} {beat}
[S01] [hook] That's odd, because you'd expect a bigger bill to be bigger every way.
[S01] [goal] You'll see what that pooled trend hides, a trap waiting in any data that mixes groups.
[S02] [roadmap] First, the pooled trend. Then, within each species. {break}

## T1 · The pooled trend

[S03] [announce] First, the pooled trend.
[S03] [introduce] Pooled means we ignore species. These birds come from three islands in the Palmer Archipelago, Antarctica, measured from twenty oh seven to twenty oh nine. {C10}
[S03] [explain] Across is bill length. Up is bill depth, how tall the bill is. {look:2}
[S04] [explain] Here's every bird, with the line that fits them best. {C02} {look:3}
[S04] [explain] It tilts down. The correlation is minus point two four: weak, but the p-value is tiny. {C01} {beat}
[S05] [wrap-up] Pooled, longer bills go with shallower bills. {C01} {look:2}
[S05] [bridge] But these birds belong to three species, and a pooled line can hide what each group does. {C03} {break}

## T2 · Within each species

[S06] [announce] So now, within each species.
[S06] [introduce] Does the downward tilt hold for Adelie, Chinstrap, and Gentoo penguins separately? {C03}
[S06] [explain] Suppose length and depth trade off. Then every species tilts down.
[S06] [explain] Suppose instead long and deep go together. Then, inside a species, longer bills are deeper.
[S06] [predict] Make your call. Inside each species, up, or down? {look:4}
[S07] [reveal] Every species tilts *up*. Adelie, point three nine. Chinstrap, point six five. Gentoo, point six four. {C04} {C05} {C06} {beat}
[S07] [explain] If you said down, the pooled line misled you. Each species trend is stronger, and opposite. {C01} {C04} {C05} {C06}
[S08] [explain] Here's why the pooled line tilts down: look at where the groups sit.
[S08] [explain] Adelies sit left, with short, deep bills. Chinstraps sit top right, long and deep. {C07} {C09} {beat}
[S08] [explain] Gentoos sit bottom right: long bills, but the shallowest, about fifteen millimeters. {C08} {beat}
[S08] [explain] One line through everyone runs from short, deep bills down to long, shallow Gentoo bills. {C07} {C08} {look:2}
[S09] [wrap-up] Within each species, longer bills are deeper. The pooled tilt comes from mixing groups. This reversal is called Simpson's paradox. {C04} {C05} {C06} {look:2}
[S09] [bridge] Now we can answer the opening puzzle: why longer went with shallower. {break}

## Conclusion

[S10] [synthesis] Pooled, the trend tilts down. Within each species, it tilts up. Both are true of the same birds. {C01} {C04} {C05} {C06}
[S10] [reveal] So inside a species, longer bills do tend to be deeper. Gentoos pair long bills with shallow ones, and that tilts the pooled line down. {C08} {beat}
[S10] [unknown] These are correlations, and these data don't include diet, so we can't say why each bill has its shape. {C11} {look:2}
