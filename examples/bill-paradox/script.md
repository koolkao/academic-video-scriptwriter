# Script: Longer bills, shallower bills?

## Opening

[S01] [hook] Across three hundred and forty two penguins, birds with longer bills tend to have shallower bills. Look inside any one species, and the opposite is true. {C01} {C02} {beat}
[S01] [goal] By the end, you will be able to read the figures that show this, and explain how both statements hold at once.
[S01] [roadmap] We will go step by step: reading a contour plot, the pooled trend, splitting by species, species profiles, and every pair of measurements. {break}

## T1 · Reading a contour plot

[S02] [announce] First: reading a contour plot.
[S02] [introduce] A contour plot is a map of where observations crowd together, drawn with rings like the height lines on a hiking map.
[S03] [explain] Across the bottom is bill length, in millimeters. Up the side is bill depth: how tall the bill is where it meets the head. {look:2}
[S03] [explain] This one ring joins every place with the same density of birds. {beat}
[S03] [explain] Now all the rings. Where they stack into a peak, many birds share those measurements.
[S04] [wrap-up] So a contour plot does not show single birds. It shows where birds are common. Unlike a hiking map, the height here is a count of birds, not ground. {look:2}
[S04] [bridge] Now that we can read the map, we can ask what it says about bills overall. {break}

## T2 · The pooled trend

[S05] [announce+introduce] Next: the pooled trend. Pooled means all three species together, treated as one population. {C01}
[S06] [explain] Follow the rings from left to right. As bills get longer, they tend to get shallower. {look:2}
[S06] [explain] The correlation is minus point two four, across three hundred and forty two birds. Weak, but clearly negative. {C02} {beat}
[S07] [wrap-up] Taken together, the penguins say: longer bill, shallower bill. {look:2}
[S07] [bridge] That describes the mixture. To see what the mixture is made of, we split it apart. {break}

## T3 · Splitting by species

[S08] [announce+introduce] Splitting by species. Same axes, same birds. Only now each species gets its own color.
[S09] [explain] The single landscape breaks into three islands. Adelie penguins sit at the upper left, with short, deep bills. {C11}
[S09] [explain] Chinstraps sit at the upper right, long and deep. Gentoos sit at the lower right, long and shallow. {C11} {look:2}
[S10] [explain] Now trace each island on its own. Every one tilts upward. {look:2}
[S10] [explain] Within Adelies, the correlation is point three nine. Within Chinstraps, point six five. Within Gentoos, point six four. {C03} {C04} {C05} {beat}
[S11] [wrap-up] Within *every* species, longer bills are deeper bills. The pooled trend came from where the islands sit, not from inside any of them. This reversal has a name: Simpson's paradox. {beat}
[S11] [bridge] The islands sit apart because the species differ in more than their bills. The next figure shows how. {break}

## T4 · Species profiles

[S12] [announce+introduce] Species profiles. To compare all the measurements at once, we use a radar plot.
[S12] [explain] Each spoke is one measurement: bill length, bill depth, flipper length, and body mass. The middle ring is the average penguin. Points outside it are above average, and points inside are below. {look:3}
[S12] [explain] Every spoke is measured in standard deviations, so they all share one scale.
[S13] [explain] Here is the Gentoo profile. Bill depth falls about one standard deviation below average, while flippers and body mass reach well above it. {C08} {beat}
[S13] [explain] Adelies are small birds with short but deep bills. Chinstraps have long, deep bills on smaller than average bodies. {C06} {C07} {look:2}
[S14] [wrap-up] Gentoos are big birds with long, shallow bills. Set beside short, deep Adelie bills, that is what tilts the pooled trend downward. {look:2}
[S14] [bridge] Bills are only one pair of measurements. Pooling could reverse other pairs too, so we check them all. {break}

## T5 · Every pair of measurements

[S15] [announce+introduce] Every pair of measurements. A heat map shows every correlation among the measurements in one grid.
[S15] [explain] Each cell is one pair. Warm cells are positive, cool cells are negative, and pale cells are near zero. {look:2}
[S16] [explain] Start with one cell: flipper length against body mass, all birds pooled. Point eight seven, strongly positive. {C09} {beat}
[S16] [explain] Now look along the bill depth row. Pooled, bill depth runs negative against every other measurement. {C09} {look:2}
[S17] [explain] Here is the same grid for Gentoos alone. Every cell turns warm, from point six four to point seven two. {C10} {look:3}
[S17] [wrap-up] Inside one species, deeper bills go with longer bills, longer flippers, and heavier bodies. Only the pooled grid says otherwise. {C10}
[S17] [bridge] That pattern ties the whole lesson together. {break}

## Conclusion

[S18] [synthesis] We learned to read a contour plot, and found a pooled trend: longer bills, shallower bills. Splitting by species reversed it inside every group. {C02} {C03} {C04} {C05}
[S18] [synthesis] Species profiles showed why: Gentoos pair long bills with shallow ones. And the heat map showed the same reversal for bill depth within Gentoos. {C08} {C10} {beat}
[S18] [synthesis] Both statements are true. The pooled trend describes the mixture of species. The within-species trend describes how bill length and depth go together in one kind of penguin. All of it comes from one archipelago, over three seasons, from two thousand seven to two thousand nine. {C01} {look:3}
