# Teaching plan: Which penguin is the heavy one?

## Brief

- Audience: general public, curious adults; no statistics training assumed
- Prior knowledge: knows what an average is; has not met confidence intervals
- Purpose: viewers can say which of three penguin species is heaviest and how sure the data are
- Target duration: 2:00
- Speech rate: 150 wpm
- Language: English
- Materials: examples/penguin-project (raw data and results tables)
- Engagement: full
- Assumptions: under two minutes; landscape; captions on

## Puzzle

- Question: one of three species outweighs the others by more than a kilogram. Which one, and how sure can we be?
- Rests on: C05, C06
- Answered in: T1 (which one), T2 (how sure), and the conclusion's reveal
- Predictions: T1, before the dots land ("which is the heavy one?"; all dots withheld)

## Objectives

| ID | Viewers can … | Topic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O1 | name the heaviest species and say the other two weigh about the same | T1 |
| O2 | read an estimate with its interval and say whether it could be zero | T2 |

## Topics

| ID | Title | Prerequisites | Takeaway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Weight by species | an average | Gentoos are heavy; Adelies and Chinstraps are alike. |
| T2 | The Gentoo gap | T1 | Gentoos outweigh Adelies by more than a kilogram, and the interval is far from zero. |

## Figure-reading prerequisites

A confidence interval is new to this audience. T2 introduces it in one sentence on the figure
itself (frame step), rather than as its own topic, because the video is short.

## Scope exclusions

- Sex differences within species: real and in the data, but a second idea for a one-minute video.
- The three-group ANOVA: the pairwise interval answers the viewer's question more directly.
